The path a value travels

One system, five layers

When a graphic looks wrong, walk backward through the chain. Do not start by blaming the screen.

1 · FieldA sensor, actuator, BACnet object, or Modbus register exists outside Niagara.
2 · IntegrationA Driver, Network, Device, and Proxy Extension bring it into the station.
3 · LogicSchedules, Wire Sheets, links, and components transform or command it.
4 · ServicesAlarmService and HistoryService turn behavior into records and evidence.
5 · PresentationProperty Sheets, Nav views, and Px graphics expose it to people.
Language before speed

Core vocabulary

Open a card when the term appears in a task. Learn the ownership boundary as well as the definition.

Workbench

The engineering desktop application used to connect, build, wire, and commission. Closing Workbench does not stop a station.

Station

A running Niagara application and component database on a JACE or Supervisor. Logic runs here.

Platform

The host layer beneath a station: software, modules, licenses, certificates, backup, and restart.

Component and Slot

A Component is Niagara's addressable building block. A slot is one named property, action, or topic on it.

ORD

An Object Resolution Descriptor locates a station component, slot, file, or history record unambiguously.

Proxy Extension

The binding between a Niagara point component and an external field value. It carries reads and eligible writes.

Wire Sheet and Link

The visual logic canvas and the connections that carry values from output slots to input slots.

Schedule

A component producing a time-based effective value from weekly patterns, exceptions, and defaults.

AlarmService

The service owning alarm generation, state, classes, routing, recipients, and acknowledgement behavior.

HistoryService

The service owning collected time-series records. History Extensions define what and how to collect.

Px page

A bound visual page—equipment schematic, floor plan, or dashboard—used for operator interaction.

Category, Role, Permission

Category scopes what; Permission defines the allowed verb; Role bundles permissions for a user.

DDC evidence meets mechanical reality

Troubleshoot the system, not the screen

An excellent DDC technician understands the entire cause-and-effect chain. The BAS tells you what the controller knows or believes; field measurements prove what the equipment actually did.

command → electrical output → actuator travel → fluid/air movement → heat transfer → sensor response → BAS display
Layer 01

Start with the complaint

Define what is wrong, where, when, and under what load. “Warm” is not yet a diagnosis.

Layer 02

Read the sequence

State what should be enabled, what should modulate, and which safeties can stop it.

Layer 03

Check command versus proof

A command proves intent. Status, current, travel, pressure, and flow provide separate kinds of proof.

Layer 04

Follow the energy

Trace air, water, refrigerant, heat, and electrical power. Find where the expected transfer stops.

Layer 05

Test one hypothesis

Choose the next observation that best separates competing causes. Do not fire the parts cannon.

Layer 06

Prove the repair

Remove temporary overrides, allow stabilization, verify the original symptom, and document evidence.

Safety boundary: standing water around pumps, starters, disconnects, or energized equipment is an electrical and mechanical hazard. Follow site emergency, isolation, LOTO, and escalation procedures before approaching or testing it. Training logic never authorizes unsafe field access.

Think first, then reveal

Field scenario drills

For each case, say your likely causes, the first safe checks, and the evidence that would separate them. Then open the answer. These test reasoning—not memorized fault names.

Field caseThe chiller is off on low flow. The GPM meter reads 0. The pump status says running. The pump room is full of water. What could be happening?
What the data proves

The controller commanded or proved a running state, but the flow instrument sees no circulation and the chiller's flow safety is doing its job.

Likely cause families

Major leak or failed isolation/check valve; broken coupling or damaged impeller; closed valve; lost prime/air binding; failed flow meter; or false pump-status proof.

First response

Treat the flooded room as the controlling evidence. Follow emergency isolation and LOTO procedures, identify the leak from a safe position, then verify pump rotation, valve position, differential pressure, and independent flow.

Actual finding: the valve at the pump had rusted through and was leaking. The lesson is not “zero GPM means rusty valve”; it is that run status never proves intact piping or delivered flow.

Field caseA packaged unit loses capacity and trips as load rises. A trash bag is found plastered across the outdoor coil. Explain the BAS evidence you would expect.
Physical mechanism

Blocked condenser airflow reduces heat rejection. Condensing temperature and pressure rise, compressor work rises, and available cooling capacity falls.

Likely evidence

Supply temperature drifts high, cooling command saturates, compressor capacity/current may rise, condenser approach worsens, and a high-pressure or condenser diagnostic may occur.

Proof

Inspect the coil and fan path, remove the obstruction safely, confirm coil condition and fan operation, then trend leaving temperature and capacity through recovery.

A controls-only response might retune the loop or lower a setpoint. Neither restores heat rejection. Always ask where the heat is supposed to go.

Field caseUnit 4 has excessive airflow. Space temperature is acceptable, but humidity remains high. Why can reducing airflow improve dehumidification?
Physical mechanism

At excessive airflow, coil contact time falls and the finite coil must handle more air mass. Leaving-air temperature may stay above the effective dew point, reducing latent removal.

Evidence to compare

CFM/static pressure, fan speed, entering/leaving dry bulb and RH, calculated dew point, CHW temperature/flow, valve position, and condensate production.

Do not assume

High humidity can also result from warm CHW, poor valve authority, dirty or air-bound coil, outdoor-air excess, infiltration, failed reheat sequence, or sensor bias.

The best answer connects airflow to coil capacity and dew point, then verifies competing causes. “More air is always better” is false for latent performance.

DDC signal chainThe BAS commands a CHW valve to 100% and displays 96% feedback, but supply air stays warm. What should you check next?
Leading hypotheses

Lying feedback or slipped linkage; no CHW flow; warm entering water; blocked/air-bound/fouled coil; incorrect piping; or biased temperature sensing.

High-value checks

Measure actuator output signal, physically verify stem/travel, measure entering/leaving water and air temperatures, and verify differential pressure or flow.

Core lesson

Command, feedback, physical travel, fluid flow, and heat transfer are five different facts. One cannot substitute for the others.

NiagaraA point is correct at the field controller but stale on a Px graphic. Where do you troubleshoot?
Walk the chain

Device health → Network/driver health → proxy status and tuning policy → station logic/link → Px binding ORD → Supervisor/JACE connection.

Useful clues

Proxy status, last update, fault text, device status, subscription/poll behavior, and whether the Property Sheet is stale too.

Isolation logic

If the Property Sheet is current but Px is stale, look above the proxy. If both are stale, look at the proxy, network, device, or field source.

HydronicsPump command and status are on, differential pressure is low, valves are opening, and remote coils are warm. What separates a pump problem from a system restriction?
Pump evidence

Verify speed command versus actual speed, motor current, rotation, suction/discharge pressure, coupling, impeller condition, and available suction head.

Restriction evidence

Measure pressure on both sides of strainers, valves, and suspect sections. A localized high pressure drop identifies where energy is being lost.

Reasoning

Low system DP is a symptom. Pressure distribution—not run status alone—separates weak pumping from excessive resistance or an open bypass.

ControlsA zone oscillates between hot and cold. The actuator repeatedly drives from near 0% to near 100%. Name three cause families before changing PID gains.
Mechanical

Oversized valve, poor valve authority, sticking actuator, unstable plant temperature/pressure, or excessive equipment capacity.

Sensing and timing

Bad sensor location, sensor noise/bias, fast sampling against slow thermal mass, missing actuator slew, or transport delay.

Logic

Wrong action direction, deadband error, integral windup, competing loops, schedule/override conflict, or badly tuned gains.

PID tuning is not the first move until the valve, sensor, plant, and sequence are proven capable of stable control.

CloseoutThe equipment looks normal immediately after repair. What evidence is required before closing the call?
Original symptom

Prove the complaint is resolved under a meaningful operating condition—not merely that the alarm cleared.

System recovery

Allow thermal and hydronic stabilization; trend command, feedback, process value, setpoint, and relevant upstream conditions.

Control state

Release temporary overrides, confirm normal priority/control ownership, record corrective and preventive actions, and note remaining risk.
